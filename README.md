# TSMC: The Observable Engine and the Unidentified Moat



## **5 Key Takeaways**

1. **Public evidence does not support an actionable edge at the current price.**  
   At ~$422 ADR and $2.19 tn market cap the report issues no rating and no fair value. The conclusion is about the sufficiency of available information, not whether the shares are cheap or expensive.

2. **The economic engine is mostly arithmetic, not a competitive advantage.**  
   Customer aggregation drives utilisation, which drives fixed-cost absorption (depreciation is ~42% of cost of revenue). High utilisation and falling cost per wafer are outcomes of demand, not moats. They reverse when the inputs reverse. The utilisation lever that expanded margins in 2025 is largely spent; next major capacity is 2028–2029.

3. **The real technical moat cannot be identified from public data.**  
   Yield, defect density and cost per good die are never disclosed. Four consecutive leading-edge nodes landed on schedule, but the mechanism that produces them is unobservable. No failed TSMC node exists against which alternative explanations can be tested.

4. **Today’s price requires growth that exceeds management’s own long-term objective under most discount-rate assumptions.**  
   At 9% discount rate and 3% terminal growth the model needs roughly 35% revenue CAGR through 2030 to clear the current valuation. Terminal value dominates every DCF run. Only a lower discount rate (or sustained growth well above 30%) makes the shares look supported.

5. **The main internal risk is arithmetic, not competitive failure.**  
   If revenue growth falls below the compounding depreciation schedule while capital intensity stays elevated, gross margins compress without any loss of process leadership. Overseas capacity dilution, non-operating earnings noise, and the unparameterised Taiwan discontinuity sit alongside that core mechanism.
